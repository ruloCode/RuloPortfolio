**Claude Fable 5.1** shipped a little while ago, and last night in Bogotá we filled a room to see how far it goes.

This was the **Fable 5.1 Build Day** on September 16, 2026: one night, a brand-new model, and a room full of people with their laptops open.

![A room full of builders with open laptops during the Fable 5.1 Build Day in Bogotá](/images/blog/build-day-fable-5-1-bogota/cover.jpg)

## Credits for everyone, teams formed on the spot

Every attendee got **API credits**. That changes the whole tone of an event: nobody is doing budget math before trying a dumb idea, and dumb ideas are exactly the ones that sometimes work.

Teams came together right there. You could go solo or join two, three, four people you'd met minutes earlier. I showed up without a team and left with one.

## Three tracks — and one rule I loved

The brief on screen was blunt: build whatever you want, as long as it **pushes Fable 5.1**. Then, the three tracks:

| Track | What it meant |
|---|---|
| **Delight** | Something delightful |
| **Breakthrough** | Something you couldn't build before |
| **Everyday** | Something that solves a real problem in your day |

The brilliant part is the fine print on the slide: *the tracks describe the quality of what you build, never the topic*. They don't tell you what to make. They tell you what you'll be measured against. That's the difference between a brief and a standard.

![The hosts in front of the slide with the three tracks: Delight, Breakthrough and Everyday](/images/blog/build-day-fable-5-1-bogota/tracks.jpg)

## The countdown

After planning came the clock on the projector. I caught the timer at **28:59**, and that number says more than any description of the event: there was no time for pretty architecture. There was time to decide, cut scope, and ship.

And that's when the thing I wasn't expecting happened.

## It stopped being about the model

From that point on it wasn't about the model anymore. It was **the atmosphere in the room**.

Teams that had met that same afternoon building incredible ideas, all in record time. People standing behind someone else's shoulder watching a demo come alive. That hum a room gets when everybody is making something at the same time.

A new model is an excuse. What actually produces things is a room full of people who want to build and a deadline that's an hour away.

## By the end of the night, everybody had something to show

Everybody, literally. Demos on the projector, questions on the mic, a countdown on screen.

![One of the demos projected at the front of the room](/images/blog/build-day-fable-5-1-bogota/demo.jpg)

The peak of the night was a shout from the crowd — **"¡Auto manual!"** — and the whole room breaking into applause. I can't explain the joke without the context of that demo, and that's fine: some things only land if you were there.

## What I took home

Three things:

- **Credits are the unlock.** When the API bill isn't yours, you try the weird idea. And the weird idea is the demo people remember.
- **A standard beats a topic.** "Something you couldn't build before" forces you to ask what actually changed with the new model, instead of rebuilding the same CRUD against a different API.
- **The talent in Bogotá is ready and waiting for the excuse.** All it took was a room, a projector, and one night.

Big thanks to the **Anthropic** community for opening up spaces like this in the city.

So tell me: **what would you build with a model that just shipped?**
