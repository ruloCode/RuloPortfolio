The day-in-the-life you see on social media — pretty code, coffee, a mechanical keyboard in slow motion — looks nothing like the real thing.

So I logged mine, hour by hour, exactly as it happened. An ordinary Monday.

![The setup: a meeting on one screen, the code on the other](/images/blog/dia-a-dia-ingeniero/cover.jpg)

## 7:49 a.m. — Coffee

First things first: a good coffee to start the day with the best energy. It isn't content — it's that without it the next two hours don't exist.

## 9:02 a.m. — Architecture meetings

A couple of meetings with the team to make some **architecture decisions**. This is the part of the job nobody films because there's nothing to see: two people and a diagram, deciding something that will either hurt or save you six months later.

![With the team: the part of the job that's talking, not typing](/images/blog/dia-a-dia-ingeniero/reunion.jpg)

## 9:51 a.m. — The trick: I record all my meetings

Here's the trick I apply: **I record all my meetings**. That lets me pull the action items straight out, without relying on my memory or on whatever I managed to scribble while talking.

It's the cheapest, highest-return change I've made to how I work. It isn't a spectacular agent — it's just no longer losing agreements.

## 10:18 a.m. — A freelance quote

Then I spent time preparing a **freelance quote for a real estate company in Panama**. Part of an engineer's day-to-day is, plainly, selling. Estimating scope, putting a number on it, defending it.

## 1:09 p.m. — The database migration

Together with a teammate, planning the **database migration** for one of our chatbot projects. Planning, not executing: most of a migration's risk gets resolved before you touch anything.

![Planning at the desk: the work before the work](/images/blog/dia-a-dia-ingeniero/setup.jpg)

## 4:35 p.m. — Step away from the computer

Around four in the afternoon I stepped away from the computer for a bit. Time to move.

## 5:06 p.m. — The gym

A bit of upper body. Nothing heroic: just enough to remember what going to the gym feels like.

## What the real day has that the social media day doesn't

Add up the blocks and you get something uncomfortable and true:

| Block | What it actually was |
|---|---|
| 9:02 a.m. | Talking to people |
| 9:51 a.m. | Automating my own memory |
| 10:18 a.m. | Selling |
| 1:09 p.m. | Planning before executing |

Writing code doesn't show up as its own block anywhere. Not because it doesn't exist, but because today it's spread across everything else — and because part of the work gets done by agents while I'm doing something else.

That's the real change this year, and it doesn't show up in a keyboard timelapse.

I'll be making more content about **automation and agent engineering**. If that's your thing, stick around.
