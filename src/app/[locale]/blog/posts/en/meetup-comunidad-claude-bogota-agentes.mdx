Last night was the **Claude Community Bogotá** meetup at WeWork. A full room, a single topic — **agents** — and I got to present the 3D agent office I've been building.

But before my part, what the rest of the community brought, which was the best of the night.

## The rule: the agent, running

The host opened with an idea I loved: *let the community be the ones standing up front presenting the agents*. No theory talks.

The format was simple:

- **Seven minutes** to show the agent working, on a real use case.
- **Three minutes** of questions.

With that rule there's no room to sell anyone smoke. Either the agent runs in front of everyone, or it doesn't.

## What the community brought

Every demo solved a real problem from someone's own work:

- **An agent that tests an app's flows.** The classic: the team forgot to test the login. Now an agent tests it.
- **The cost question.** One speaker put it bluntly: each run, at API prices, costs him around twenty dollars, so he runs it on his subscription, *"money I already paid."* And what he's after isn't speed, it's a little reliability on every run.
- **Agents that certify fair-trade coffee.** Someone who saw how badly agriculture needs technology and built a team of agents for the certification process.
- **An agent that finds Python courses and events for you.**
- **And one for email**, which is where most of us lose the morning.

## My turn: a 3D agent office

I opened with this question: *haven't you ever had a pile of agents, a pile of projects — work, personal — and lost track of what each one is doing?*

My idea was to put them all in one office. A replica of a WeWork inside my house, controlled with an **Xbox controller** and **by voice**.

![The 3D agent office projected on the room's screen during the meetup](/images/blog/meetup-comunidad-claude-bogota/cover.jpg)

Live, the office was empty. I asked **Hermes**, the office lead, to hire someone to show us the last video we'd edited. In walked **Iván**: *"Hi Rulo, I'm Iván."*

What happens underneath:

- **Hermes is a single voice agent with several voices.** When I ask for something, it opens a **real Claude Code session** in the project, and that session is the character you see walking around the office.
- **You see what it's doing**: the trace, the tools and the setup it has for the task.
- **And no agent runs a command with side effects without my permission.** It raises its hand, shows me the exact command and waits. Mid-demo Iván said *"I'm still waiting for your approval to run the command,"* and I had to give him the yes right there. That's the part I care about most: the human decides.

The video Iván played was a quick cut of the meetup itself, **rendered that same night**: an agent edited it with OpenMontage, from takes I'd shot in the room a little earlier.

### Credit where it's due

A couple of days before, I came across an open-source repo on Instagram called **Agent Office**, with about 150 stars. The visual base comes from there. I added two more floors, a ping-pong table, a foosball table and the connection to my real agents.

That night I published the whole project: **[Hermes OS on GitHub](https://github.com/ruloCode/hermes-os)**.

## Rock, paper, scissors

The night closed with prizes for the speakers, decided in the fairest way there is: **rock, paper, scissors**, final round included.

## What I'm taking with me

- **"Show me it running" is the best filter.** Seven minutes with the agent live say more than any deck.
- **The most interesting cases aren't about technology.** Fair-trade coffee, email, events. The agent is the tool; the problem belongs to someone else.
- **The conversation is no longer "can an agent do this?"** It's how much each run costs and how far you can trust it.

Thanks to the **Claude Community Bogotá**, to its organizers — the official Anthropic ambassadors of the Claude Code communities in Bogotá and Medellín — and to everyone who presented.
